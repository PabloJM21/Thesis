Peer Feedback on Research Proposal 

Overall, I think this is a strong and interesting proposal. The topic is clearly relevant, and the motivation for using synthetic data, semi-supervised domain adaptation, and diffusion-based style transfer is understandable. The proposal also has a good structure and the work plan seems well thought out. However, I think some sections could be made clearer and more thesis-specific. 

Introduction 

The introduction gives a good first overview of the problem, especially the limited availability of annotated real-world runway data and the Sim2Real gap. However, I felt that the beginning is still a bit broad and could provide more task-specific background. It might help to start more naturally from the aviation/ runway-detection setting: for example, why runway detection from an aircraft perspective is difficult, how weather, illumination, distance, runway perspective, and camera viewpoint affect the task, and why real annotated data is hard to obtain. 

The related work is mentioned, but I think the connection between the cited approaches and this specific thesis could be explained more clearly. In particular, I did not fully understand how diffusion models and CycleGAN-style methods would be applied in this project. A short explanation of how style transfer would change the synthetic images while preserving the runway labels would make this part easier to follow. 

Goal of the Thesis 

The overall goal is understandable, but I think one sharper sentence would help a lot. For example: 

“The goal of this thesis is to evaluate which Sim2Real strategy best improves lightweight runway-corner detection from aircraft-view images when only limited annotated real data is available.” 

This would make the research goal immediately clear before going into the details of the different approaches. 

The research gap is also present: previous work often focuses either on synthetic data, foundation models, or domain adaptation separately, while this thesis compares several Sim2Real strategies under a lightweight and reproducible setup. This is a good contribution, but it could be stated more explicitly. 

Research Objectives 

The research objectives are generally clear, but their presentation is sometimes a bit confusing because some of them read more like methodology steps than research questions. I would suggest separating the actual research questions from the planned methods. For example, first state the question, then explain which experiment will answer it. 

1

It would also be useful to clarify whether the YOLO-pose model will be trained from scratch or fine-tuned from an existing checkpoint. This is important because it affects reproducibility, training time, and the interpretation of the results. 

Since the proposal focuses on lightweight and edge-deployable models, it might also be worth briefly justifying why YOLO-pose is the main choice. Maybe other real-time detector families, such as RF-DETR or RT-DETR-style models, could be mentioned as possible alternatives or baselines, even if they are not the main focus. This would make the model choice feel more deliberate. 

Expected Results and Contributions 

The expected results are understandable, especially the expectation that the baseline will show a Sim2Real gap and that domain adaptation or diffusion-based style transfer may improve performance. However, the expected results are not yet very quantitatively meaningful. 

I would suggest adding clearer evaluation targets or at least naming the main metrics. For example, will the main metric be mAP, mIoU, keypoint error, OKS, inference speed, or a combination of these? Since the task is runway corner detection, it would be helpful to explain which metric best reflects successful runway detection in practice. 

It would also help to specify what would count as a meaningful improvement. For example, is an improvement of 2–3 mAP points already useful, or is the expectation a larger gain over the synthetic-only baseline? Even rough quantitative expectations would make the expected contribution stronger. 

Methodology 

The methodology is one of the stronger parts of the proposal. The work packages are clearly listed, and the stages follow a logical order: dataset filtering, baseline training, semi-supervised domain adaptation, diffusion style transfer, and final comparison. 

However, some parts could still be clarified. For example, the pseudo-labeling process should explain how “high-confidence” pseudo-labels are selected and what threshold or filtering strategy will be used. Similarly, for the diffusion stage, it would be useful to explain how the method will ensure that the runway geometry and corner labels remain valid after style transfer. 

The evaluation plan is good because it proposes comparing models across synthetic subsets and a real world test set. Still, the proposal should more clearly state how the final comparison will be performed and which metrics will decide the best model. 

Work Plan and Feasibility 

The work plan is good and gives a realistic overview of the main tasks. The stages are concrete, and the buffer at the end is a good idea. However, the proposal might benefit from a short risk-management note. For example, diffusion model setup, YouTube frame filtering, and iterative self-training could take longer than expected. It would be useful to mention what the minimum viable thesis outcome would be if one of the more complex stages takes too much time.
